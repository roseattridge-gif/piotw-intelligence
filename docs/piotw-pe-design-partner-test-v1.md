# PIOTW PE design-partner test v1

## Purpose

Use this guide for a 30–40 minute, neutral customer test with a PE operating partner or investment professional. Demonstrate the factual prototype; do not sell the future prediction vision before observing how the participant uses what exists.

## Opening

“PIOTW is an experimental outside-in company-monitoring product that consolidates factual changes and links every claim back to its source.”

State that the prototype does not yet assess risk, importance, company health or future outcomes. Ask the participant to think aloud. Avoid explaining where to click unless they are blocked.

## Tasks

1. Open the portfolio brief. Ask: “What do you think this is telling you?”
2. Ask the participant to choose one company and find what changed since the previous observation.
3. Ask them to compare two companies using only the available factual information.
4. Ask them to verify one factual item against its exact evidence and original source.
5. Ask: “If this arrived before a portfolio review or investment discussion, what—if anything—would you do with it?”

Record where they hesitate, what they ignore, what they investigate, the language they use, and whether they can distinguish observation from interpretation without prompting.

## Interview questions

- What, if anything, was useful?
- What was confusing or misleading?
- What would make you return every week?
- What information is missing?
- What would you use instead today?
- Who inside your organisation would use this?
- What decision or conversation could this inform?
- Would you pay for PIOTW in its current factual-only form? Why or why not?
- What would it need to do for you to pay?
- Which evidence did you trust most and least?
- Did the source links materially change your trust?

Do not ask whether they “want AI predictions” or suggest scores as the solution. If they independently request interpretation, ranking or alerts, capture their exact words and the decision they are trying to make.

## Closing capture

Immediately after the session record:

- participant role and firm type;
- portfolio/target monitoring responsibility;
- tasks completed without help;
- strongest useful moment;
- largest confusion or trust failure;
- current alternative workflow;
- stated return frequency;
- purchase signal, objection and required capability;
- verbatim phrases worth preserving;
- recommended product change, if supported by observed behaviour.

Do not turn one interview into a roadmap. Compare patterns after 3–5 target-user sessions.
