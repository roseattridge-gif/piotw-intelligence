# PIOTW MVP hypotheses v1

Status: **CUSTOMER-DISCOVERY HYPOTHESES — NOT VALIDATED CLAIMS**

| Hypothesis | Evidence that would support it | Evidence that would falsify it | Customer question or task |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 — PE users value a consolidated outside-in factual change feed. | Participants independently use the feed to orient themselves, identify a company to inspect and describe a recurring monitoring use. | They ignore the feed, cannot distinguish it from existing alerts, or say consolidation saves no meaningful time. | “Starting from the portfolio brief, show me what you would inspect first and why.” |
| H2 — Evidence traceability materially increases trust. | Participants open evidence references, use them to verify a claim and say provenance makes the output usable or shareable. | They do not inspect sources, do not understand the evidence chain, or still distrust a correctly evidenced fact. | “Choose one claim you would repeat to a colleague and verify it.” |
| H3 — Careers movement is useful before predictive interpretation. | Participants identify a legitimate monitoring question or follow-up action from factual hiring lifecycle changes without asking PIOTW to label them good or bad. | They regard vacancy facts as noise, too easy to misread or unusable without an interpretation layer. | “What, if anything, could these careers movements help you investigate?” |
| H4 — Users want cross-company factual comparison. | Participants compare companies voluntarily, understand differing coverage and describe portfolio-review or screening use. | Comparison creates false equivalence, is ignored, or users insist only company-specific context is useful. | “Compare two companies and explain what you can and cannot conclude.” |
| H5 — Users can identify actions from facts without a PIOTW score. | Participants name a sensible next question, document to read or colleague to contact while respecting the factual limits. | They cannot decide what to inspect without rank/score, or infer unsupported risk despite the boundaries. | “What would you do next with this information?” |
| H6 — A concise portfolio/company brief is shareable inside a PE workflow. | Participants say they would use or forward the brief before a portfolio review, IC discussion or operating call and can identify the recipient. | The brief is too long, too sparse, poorly timed or unsuitable for internal circulation. | “Who would receive this, in what meeting, and in what format?” |
| H7 — Explicit gaps increase credibility rather than reduce perceived value. | Participants notice coverage limitations and say they prefer visible missingness to implied completeness. | Gaps make the product feel unusable or participants fail to understand what PIOTW cannot see. | “Where is PIOTW blind here, and does that change how you would use the brief?” |

## Evidence standard

Treat observed task behaviour, specific current workflows, willingness to introduce a colleague, willingness to trial with real portfolio names, and concrete budget/process detail as stronger evidence than general enthusiasm. Treat “interesting,” hypothetical feature requests and polite praise as weak evidence.

After 3–5 interviews, review each hypothesis as supported, mixed, unsupported or not tested. Preserve contradictory evidence. Do not add product analytics, scores or prediction features merely to make a hypothesis easier to confirm.
